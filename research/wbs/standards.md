# WBS rules: authoritative basis and provenance

Research date: 11 Nov 2026. Web research only. Verdicts: **sourced** = in a standard or government/peer-reviewed document I could verify; **directional** = reputable named book or widely repeated by reputable bodies, original not verified by me; **folklore** = no traceable origin.

## Headline findings

1. **The 8/80 rule has no standards basis I could find.** It is absent from the NASA, GAO and DOE WBS documents (I searched their full text) and ANSI/EIA-748 says outright that work-package size and duration "will vary subject to internal management needs". Every attribution to "PMI" or "PMBOK" I found was on a blog or exam-prep site.
2. **The only sourced work-package size number I found is GAO's "typically 4 to 6 weeks in duration"**, which is far larger than 80 hours for a team.
3. **The "reporting period rule" is real but is about schedule activities, not work packages** (GAO Schedule Assessment Guide).
4. **The 100% rule is solidly sourced as a rule; the Haugan-coined-it claim is only directional.** The idea predates his 2002 book (PMBOK 2000, EIA-748 1998).
5. **"Deliverable-oriented" is a US/PMI position, not universal.** APM defines the WBS as the work, and puts deliverables in a separate product breakdown structure. PMBOK has long allowed phases at the top level; NASA and DOE call that an error.
6. **I could not read the body text of four key documents**: PMI Practice Standard for WBS 3rd ed., PMBOK 6/7/8, ISO 21511:2018, MIL-STD-881F (paywalled or blocked). Claims about them are marked secondhand.

**Most defensible generic building WBS top level**: an element-based structure (UniFormat II groups A-G, or NRM 1 group elements 1-8 for UK-style users) plus common elements for project management, design and preliminaries. Reasons: it satisfies the product-oriented rule in NASA, GAO and DOE; DOE's own Buildings WBS is built on UniFormat II; NRM 1 calls itself an elemental WBS; the UniFormat II headings are citable from a public NIST report. Treat MasterFormat as a lower-level or procurement view (NRM 1 describes reallocating elements "to work packages for tendering purposes"). Treat RIBA stages as a schedule or gate dimension, not WBS branches, if the tool claims to follow the product-oriented rule.

## (a) Safe to enforce as checks

1. Children account for all of the parent's work, nothing extra (100% rule). Cite GAO-20-195G p. 58 and DOE WBS Handbook s.3.2.
2. Each element has exactly one parent; no scope in two elements. Cite NASA s.2.2; GAO-20-195G p. 65.
3. At least three levels. Cite GAO-20-195G p. 57.
4. A project management element exists at Level 2. Cite GAO-20-195G p. 61; NASA App. C.
5. Every element has a unique hierarchical code. Cite NASA s.3.4.2.
6. Every element has a dictionary entry. Cite GAO-20-195G p. 69; NASA s.3.4.4.
7. Flag Level 2 names that are phases, departments, skill types or cost classes. Cite NASA s.3.5.2; GAO-20-195G pp. 59-60. Make this switchable: PMBOK and APM permit phase- or work-based structures.
8. Each work package has one accountable owner. Cite GAO-20-195G p. 225 (control account manager) and ISO 21502 clause 4.5.8 (role exists; text unread).
9. Dependencies are stored against activities or as schedule logic, not as WBS structure. Cite GAO-20-195G pp. 60, 65.
10. Warn on start-to-finish links. Cite GAO-16-89G.
11. Warn if an "Other" element exceeds 10% of its level. Cite DOE s.3.2 (single-agency rule).

## (b) Show only as rules of thumb

- 8/80 hours: label "common practitioner heuristic, no standards source".
- GAO's "typically 4 to 6 weeks" for work packages: sourced, but descriptive.
- 4% rule: "Heerkens 2002, as reported".
- Reporting-period rule: label it as schedule-activity guidance (GAO-16-89G p. 63).
- Depth of 3 to 6 levels; each parent has two or more children.
- All estimate ranges. Suggested label for sample hours: "illustrative, concept-stage figures; for comparison, AACE gives -20% to -50% / +30% to +100% for Class 5 process-industry cost estimates, and no equivalent exists for software effort".
- 100% rule "coined by Haugan (2002)": say "attributed to".

## (c) Drop

- The "40-hour rule" and the "if it makes sense" rule.
- Any wording that says PMI or PMBOK "requires" 8/80.
- "Two to four levels" as a rule.
- "Mutually exclusive elements" as a quoted standard phrase; use the NASA/GAO wording.
