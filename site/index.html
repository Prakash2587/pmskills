The gap isn't reporting. It's the join.

We read several hundred threads across the Atlassian, Asana, monday.com, ClickUp and Smartsheet communities, plus Hacker News and practitioner writing, looking for what people actually complain about in project cost and capacity.

The same shape came back everywhere. Nobody is asking for a better Gantt chart. They are asking for one thing no tool gives them:

time entries→cost rate→ budget→client→across projects

So they rebuild it in a spreadsheet. One team embeds a Google Sheet inside their PM tool just to see how much budget is left. Zoho Projects already holds every side of that join — logged hours, per-person cost rates, project budget, client, and its own capacity verdict. These skills exist to make that fact useful.

Weekly status report

Next

Turns raw project state into a RAG update shaped for whoever reads it. Classifies each task as shipped, in flight, slipped or blocked, and posts to the project feed on approval.

The single most recurring theme in PM communities: format, cadence, and the complaint that stakeholders don't read what gets sent.

Stale-task chaser

Next

Finds work going quietly sideways — past due and silent, in progress but untouched, stuck waiting — and drafts a peer-tone nudge per owner. Never chases the same person more than five times in a run.

Highest value-to-effort ratio of any skill: computable from timestamps alone, with near-zero false-positive risk.

How these are built

Project management is full of numbers everyone repeats and nobody can source. We checked them against PMI, AACE International, APM, ISO 21502 and the published benchmark studies before writing a line of logic. Most did not survive:

  • The 85% utilisation target has no authoritative source and is directly refuted in the literature. It is not used here.
  • Sector utilisation bands for agencies and MSPs trace only to software vendors' blogs. They are reported as having no published benchmark, never as a target you are failing.
  • No standards body prescribes a contingency percentage, so none is suggested.
  • Weekend-work findings cite published productivity research, and only fire on a sustained pattern — a single heavy week carries no evidenced penalty.

Every finding is graded hard evidence, derived or inference, and the tool states plainly what it cannot see.
